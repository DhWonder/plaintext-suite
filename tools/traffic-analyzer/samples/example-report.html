What is happening

About 98% of all traffic in this capture is hostile. There is more than one thing going on at once here: a SYN flood, which fills up the connection table so real customers cannot get in, a reflection attack bounced off DNS and NTP servers, which multiplies the attacker's bandwidth against you, a ping flood, and finally at least one host scanning your ports, which is a separate problem from the flood and worth looking at once the dust settles. It is arriving from many addresses at the same time, which is what makes it distributed, and why blocking addresses by hand as they appear will never catch up.

What to change

highTurn on SYN cookies and connection rate limits

Your server is holding open thousands of half finished connections. Enabling SYN cookies lets it answer without reserving memory for each one, and a connection rate limit per address stops any single source hogging the queue. This is a configuration change, not new hardware.

highPut the website behind a filtering service

A service like Cloudflare absorbs this kind of flood before it reaches your line. For a small business the entry plans cost little and this is the single most effective change available.

highAsk your provider to filter reflected DNS, NTP traffic upstream

Reflected traffic is already using up your line before you can drop it, so filtering has to happen upstream at your internet provider. Send them the source list from this report and ask for a filter on that traffic.

mediumCheck that none of your own servers can be used this way

The same services being abused against you (DNS, NTP) are often left open on other people's networks by accident. Any of these running on your side should only answer to your own addresses, never to the whole internet.

lowRate limit ping traffic

Ping is useful for diagnostics, so rather than blocking it completely, limit it to a few packets a second. That keeps it working for you and useless for them.

mediumLook at what the scanner found, not just that it scanned

One host walked across your ports looking for a way in. Scanning is constant background noise on the internet, so the useful question is what it would have found. Anything answering that does not need to face the internet should be closed or moved behind a VPN.

mediumConsider limiting traffic from APNIC (Asia Pacific) while the attack is running

Most of the hostile traffic was allocated in APNIC (Asia Pacific). If your customers are not there, a temporary geographic limit is a quick way to cut the volume. Check your real customer list first, and treat it as a temporary measure rather than a permanent policy.

lowWrite down what normal looks like

The reason this analysis works is that we can compare against ordinary traffic. Keeping a baseline of your typical packet rates and busiest hours means the next incident gets diagnosed in minutes instead of hours.

lowAgree who calls the provider, before the next time

In an attack, most of the delay is not technical. It is working out who is allowed to phone the internet provider and authorise emergency filtering. Decide that now and write the number on the emergency sheet.
